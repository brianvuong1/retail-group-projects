CPP Farm Store — Omnichannel & Ecommerce Performance Dashboard

Author

Kevin, Alexa, Sandy, Brian, Ron

Published

August 2, 2026

Purpose

The purpose of this assignment is to design a performance dashboard that shows how CPP Farm Store could evaluate its omnichannel and ecommerce efforts. The dashboard connects campaign goals with measurable outcomes.

This dashboard uses simulated data, clearly labeled as such, since real CPP Farm Store data are not available. The goal is to demonstrate what should be measured and how results should guide decision-making.

Key Question: How should CPP Farm Store measure the success of its omnichannel campaign and ecommerce efforts?

Step 1: Dashboard Users (Alexa)

The CPP Farm Store dashboard is designed primarily for the CPP Farm Store manager, marketing team, ecommerce manager, and student consulting team. These users need a clear overview of campaign performance, website activity, product interest, and customer behavior across digital and in-store channels.

The CPP Farm Store manager and client leadership can use the dashboard to review overall performance and identify whether the omnichannel campaign is supporting business goals. The marketing team can use it to compare traffic sources, evaluate campaign content, and determine which channels are generating the most engagement. The ecommerce manager can monitor product page activity, shopping behavior, and conversion-related actions. The student consulting team can use the dashboard to analyze results and develop recommendations for future campaigns.

Because the dashboard serves several users, it should provide both high-level performance summaries and more detailed channel, product, and customer behavior information.

2. Measurement Objectives (Alexa)

The main objective of the dashboard is to help CPP Farm Store evaluate the success of its omnichannel campaign and ecommerce efforts. The dashboard connects campaign activities with measurable customer actions so that the Farm Store can determine what is performing well and where improvements are needed.

The dashboard will be designed to:

  • Measure website traffic and changes in visitor activity over time to understand overall interest in the Farm Store and identify trends in customer engagement.

  • Identify which marketing channels generate the most users and sessions to determine where visitors are coming from and which channels drive the most traffic.

  • Compare traffic from social media, email, search, direct visits, and other campaign sources to evaluate the effectiveness of each marketing channel.

  • Evaluate customer engagement with product pages and campaign content by monitoring how visitors interact with featured products and promotional materials.

  • Measure interest in featured CPP Farm Store products to identify which items attract the most attention and support merchandising decisions.

  • Track customer actions such as add-to-cart events, checkout starts, purchases, email signups, and store pickup interest to measure how customers progress through the buying journey.

  • Compare new and returning visitor behavior to better understand customer acquisition, retention, and repeat engagement.

  • Identify the products, campaigns, and channels that contribute most to conversions or simulated revenue to help prioritize future marketing efforts and promotional strategies.

  • Provide insights that help CPP Farm Store improve future marketing, ecommerce, and omnichannel decisions by using performance data to guide strategic planning and continuous improvement.

These objectives support the campaign plan by showing whether digital marketing activities are increasing awareness, encouraging product exploration, and motivating customers to take meaningful actions online or visit the physical store.

Step 3: KPIs (Ron)

KPIs were selected to directly answer the measurement objectives and support the dashboard users identified for this project. Every metric included below answers a specific business question for a specific user — no KPI was added simply because it was available.

3.1 Executive Summary KPIs

For: CPP Farm Store manager and client leadership

KPI Business Question Answered
Users Is overall interest in CPP Farm Store growing?
Sessions How much total activity is the campaign generating?
Purchases (simulated) Is the campaign driving buying behavior?
Revenue (simulated) What is the campaign’s monetary impact?
Conversion Rate How efficiently is traffic turning into action?
Average Order Value Are customers buying more per transaction?

These six metrics give leadership a quick read on whether the campaign is working at the highest level without requiring them to dig into channel or product details.

3.2 Traffic Acquisition KPIs

For: Marketing team

KPI Business Question Answered
Traffic Source Which channels — social, email, search, direct — are driving visits?
Sessions by Channel Where is the most engagement coming from?
Returning Visitors Is the store building repeat engagement over the campaign?
New vs. Returning Split Is the campaign reaching new audiences or re-engaging existing ones?

These metrics support the marketing team’s need to compare channel effectiveness and determine where to invest future campaign efforts.

3.3 Product Performance KPIs

For: Ecommerce manager

KPI Business Question Answered
Product Page Views Which products are generating the most interest?
Add-to-Cart Events Is interest in a product converting to purchase intent?
Purchases by Product Which products are actually selling?

These three metrics form a mini-funnel at the product level — from awareness (views) to intent (add-to-cart) to action (purchase) — giving the ecommerce manager a clear picture of which products are performing and which are losing customers somewhere along the way.

3.4 Campaign Performance KPIs

For: Marketing team and campaign manager

KPI Business Question Answered
Campaign Clicks Is the campaign creative getting attention?
Click-Through Rate (CTR) Is the targeting and messaging relevant to the audience?

CTR is especially important here because a low CTR signals a mismatch between who is seeing the ad and what the ad is saying — a directly actionable insight for the campaign manager.

3.5 Customer Behavior Funnel KPIs

For: Ecommerce manager and student consulting team

KPI Business Question Answered
Sessions → Product Views Are visitors engaging with products at all?
Product Views → Add to Cart Are interested visitors moving toward purchase?
Add to Cart → Checkout Started Are shoppers following through on intent?
Checkout Started → Purchases Are shoppers completing the transaction?
Overall Conversion Rate How efficient is the full funnel end to end?

Each step in this funnel is a KPI in its own right because each drop-off point tells a different story. A drop between sessions and product views suggests a navigation or homepage issue. A drop between add-to-cart and checkout suggests friction at the cart level. The consulting team can use this to pinpoint exactly where CPP Farm Store should focus improvement efforts.

3.6 Omnichannel Signal KPI

For: CPP Farm Store manager and student consulting team

KPI Business Question Answered
Store Pickup Interest Is the online campaign successfully driving customers to the physical store?

This is the single most important KPI for evaluating whether CPP Farm Store’s omnichannel strategy is working. All other KPIs measure digital performance — this one measures whether digital activity is translating into real-world behavior, which is the core goal of an omnichannel campaign.

Step 4: Data Sources (Ron)

Because CPP Farm Store does not have a live ecommerce operation with real transaction history, all data used in this dashboard is simulated and clearly labeled as such throughout. Each dataset was built to realistically reflect what a farm store of this size and scope would see during a short omnichannel campaign. Values were modeled using industry benchmarks for small regional retailers where noted.

4.2 Data Sources Explained

GA4 — Simulated Website traffic metrics including users, sessions, traffic source breakdowns, product page views, add-to-cart events, checkout starts, and returning visitor behavior are drawn from a simulated GA4 dataset. The data covers a 30-day campaign window and reflects traffic split across organic search, direct, social, and email channels using retail industry benchmarks as a reference point.

Shopify — Simulated Product-level performance data including product page views, add-to-cart events, purchases, revenue, and average order value is sourced from a simulated Shopify export. Products included are based on CPP Farm Store’s known offerings such as produce, eggs, honey, and seasonal goods. Pricing and purchase volume were estimated to reflect realistic basket sizes for a campus farm store.

Google Ads — Simulated Campaign click and click-through rate data is drawn from a simulated Google Ads export representing a small paid search and Shopping Ads campaign running alongside organic and social efforts. CTR values were benchmarked against Google’s published retail industry averages of approximately 2–4% for Shopping Ads.

Social Media Metrics — Simulated Traffic driven from Instagram and Facebook is reflected in the GA4 traffic source breakdown. Engagement figures such as reach and link clicks were estimated based on a farm store audience of 1,000–3,000 followers using typical organic reach rates for small business accounts.

Email Campaign Metrics — Simulated Email-driven sessions are included in the traffic acquisition section. Open rates and click-through rates were modeled using Mailchimp’s retail benchmark averages of approximately 21% open rate and 2.5% CTR.

Store Pickup Interest — Custom Mock Dataset Because store pickup is the core omnichannel signal for CPP Farm Store, a custom dataset was created to simulate pickup-related behavior — including which channel visitors came from before expressing pickup interest and how that interest varies by product category. This dataset has no real-world equivalent and is entirely simulated.

4.3 Data Limitations

WarningSimulated Data Notice

All data in this dashboard is simulated and does not represent actual CPP Farm Store transaction history, web analytics, or campaign results. The purpose is to demonstrate what should be measured and how it should guide decisions. If CPP Farm Store were to implement this dashboard operationally, real GA4, Shopify, and Google Ads accounts would replace all simulated figures with live data.

A few additional limitations worth noting:

  • No historical baseline. Without prior campaign data, year-over-year or campaign-over-campaign comparisons are not possible. Industry averages are used as benchmarks instead.
  • Store pickup is a proxy signal. Pickup interest cannot confirm whether a customer actually visited the physical store after browsing online.
  • Attribution gaps. In a real deployment, some conversions would be difficult to attribute to a single channel — particularly for customers who discovered the store on social media but purchased through a direct visit.

Step 5: Design Dashboard Sections (Brian)

To evaluate CPP Farm Store’s omnichannel and ecommerce campaign, the dashboard is organized into six sections, each targeted at a specific measurement objective and audience.

5.1 Executive Summary

High-level scorecards (Users, Sessions, Purchases, Revenue, Conversion Rate, AOV) for CPP Farm Store leadership to get a quick read on overall campaign health.

5.2 Traffic Acquisition

Session/user trends over time and channel comparison, for the marketing team to see where visitors are coming from and whether traffic is growing.

5.3 Product Performance

Page views, add-to-cart, and purchases by product, for the ecommerce manager to identify which products are generating interest and which are underperforming.

5.4 Campaign Performance

Clicks and click-through rate by paid/owned channel, for the campaign manager to evaluate creative and targeting effectiveness.

5.5 Customer Behavior Funnel

Sessions → Product Views → Add to Cart → Checkout Started → Purchases, to show where drop-off occurs in the conversion path.

5.6 Store Pickup Interest (Omnichannel Signal)

Pickup interest by channel, to measure how well the online experience is driving in-store engagement — the core omnichannel signal for this dashboard.

NoteSection Rationale

Each section maps directly to a measurement objective from Step 2 and a KPI from Step 3 — no section or chart type was added without a clear business question behind it. Sections are ordered from highest-level (Executive Summary) to most granular (Store Pickup Interest) so leadership can skim the top while operational teams can dig into specifics further down.

Step 6: Build Visuals (Kevin)

The dashboard was created in Looker Studio using simulated Google Analytics 4 (GA4) and ecommerce data for the CPP Farm Store. The goal of the dashboard is to provide a clear, organized view of campaign performance so managers and the student consulting team can quickly evaluate website activity, customer engagement, product performance, and overall marketing effectiveness.

The dashboard was designed using a combination of scorecards, charts, and tables to present the most important performance metrics without overwhelming the user. Each visualization answers a specific business question and supports the campaign objectives identified in the previous steps.

Dashboard Visualizations

Executive Summary

The Executive Summary uses scorecards to provide an overview of the campaign’s key performance indicators (KPIs). These include:

  • Total Users
  • Sessions
  • Purchases
  • Revenue
  • Product Views
  • Store Pickup Interest

These metrics allow CPP Farm Store managers to immediately evaluate the overall success of the campaign.

Website Traffic

A line chart titled Website Sessions Over Time displays changes in website traffic throughout the campaign period. This visualization helps identify trends in visitor activity and whether marketing efforts are increasing website engagement over time.

Marketing Channel Performance

A bar chart compares website sessions across marketing channels, including Social Media, Organic Search, Paid Search, Email, and Direct traffic. This visualization helps determine which channels generate the most website traffic and supports future marketing budget decisions.

Product Performance

Product performance is presented using both a horizontal bar chart and a performance table.

The horizontal bar chart compares product page views across CPP Farm Store products, while the accompanying table summarizes:

  • Product Views
  • Add-to-Cart Events
  • Checkout Starts
  • Purchases
  • Revenue

These visuals help identify the products generating the greatest customer interest and those contributing the most simulated revenue.

Campaign Performance

Campaign performance is displayed through a bar chart showing Purchases by Campaign and a detailed summary table containing:

  • Sessions
  • Purchases
  • Revenue

These visuals allow the marketing team to compare campaign effectiveness and determine which promotional efforts produce the strongest customer response.

Step 7: Interpretation & Recommendations (Sandy)

NoteWhat is performing well?

Organic Search and Email show the strongest session-to-purchase efficiency in the simulated data, suggesting existing SEO content and the email list are healthy assets worth reinforcing.

WarningWhat needs improvement?

Paid Social shows comparatively high clicks but a lower simulated conversion rate — indicating a possible mismatch between ad creative/targeting and landing page relevance.

TipWhich product or channel deserves more attention?

The Seasonal Produce Bundle and Farm Box - Large show high page views but comparatively lower conversion, suggesting an opportunity to test pricing, bundling, or product-page content improvements.

ImportantWhat should CPP Farm Store do next?
  1. Reallocate a portion of Paid Social budget toward Email and Organic Search, which show stronger simulated efficiency.
  2. A/B test product page content for high-traffic, low-conversion items.
  3. Promote store pickup more prominently at checkout, since simulated pickup interest is a meaningful share of purchases — supporting the omnichannel strategy.
  4. Build a returning-visitor nurture flow (email/loyalty) since repeat visits are a leading indicator of retention.
