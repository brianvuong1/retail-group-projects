Final Report: CPP Farm Store Omnichannel Strategy

Author

Kevin, Alexa, Sandy, Brian, Ron Sabilla

Published

August 9, 2026

1. Executive Summary

Client Problem or Opportunity: CPP Farm Store is a student-run specialty retail store on the Cal Poly Pomona campus that sells fresh produce, estate wine, plants, honey, and custom gift items that are all grown or made on site. It has a unique story and high-quality products, but it faces a problem that’s hard to ignore: most people don’t know it exists. Off-campus locals, alumni, and even students on campus often have no idea the store is open to the public, and the ones who do find the website confusing to use.

Main Recommendation: Build a connected omnichannel strategy that bridges the gap between the physical store and the digital experience. That means improving the Shopify store, running a focused campaign around the custom gift box feature, setting up Google Merchant Center so products show up in search, and adding loyalty and email tools to keep customers coming back after their first purchase.

Key Supporting Evidence: We analyzed the customer journey across five different shopper segments, reviewed competitor strategies from local farm stores and specialty food retailers, audited CPP Farm Store’s current Shopify and social media presence, and built out a campaign plan targeting the store’s most underperforming — but highest-potential — product: the customizable gift box. What we found across all of that work was the same issue showing up at every stage: customers drop off between Awareness and Consideration because there’s not enough product information online, checkout is unclear, and there’s zero communication after a purchase.

Expected Benefits: A 40% increase in custom gift box orders during peak gifting seasons, a 25% jump in product page traffic, a 3% online conversion rate, and a growing base of repeat customers — all while giving student operators more hands-on experience running a real digital retail operation.

2. Client and Situation Analysis

Target Customers: CPP Farm Store has five main customer groups, and each one shops a little differently.

CPP Students are the most convenient audience — they’re already on campus every day. They care about affordability and locally made products and spend a lot of time on Instagram and TikTok. The problem is that a lot of them don’t even know the store exists. With some awareness and better digital access, this group has serious potential for consistent, repeat purchases.

Faculty and Staff are already showing up. They’re reliable shoppers who value convenience and knowing that the store will have what they want when they come in. They’re also the easiest segment to keep loyal.

Local Community Members are off-campus shoppers who typically find the store through Google, Google Maps, or a friend’s recommendation. They’re interested in local food, but they run into real friction: the campus is hard to navigate, parking is unclear, and the website doesn’t make it obvious that the store is open to everyone.

Alumni are one of the most valuable groups, even if they’re the hardest to reach. They have a strong emotional connection to CPP and love buying gifts tied to the campus, especially for holidays and special occasions. Since most of them live off campus, they depend almost entirely on the website, email, and social media to stay connected. The custom gift box was practically made for this segment.

Online-First Shoppers want to browse, order, and pay online before ever stepping foot on campus. They expect a smooth e-commerce experience: good product photos, clear pricing, easy checkout, and a straightforward pickup process.

Product Categories:

  • Fresh produce and agricultural goods: seasonal fruits, vegetables, eggs, and honey — the foundation of the store’s farm-fresh identity and the core of what brings regular shoppers in
  • Specialty and value-added products: estate wine from the campus vineyard, student-made ice cream, jams, and other preserved goods that tend to surprise and delight customers, setting the store apart from a regular grocery run
  • Nursery and ornamentals: plants and seasonal items that appeal mostly to local community members and faculty looking for something a little different
  • Gift products: customizable gift boxes and curated fruit packs — the store’s highest-margin category and the main focus of our campaign strategy

Current Strengths: CPP Farm Store’s biggest strength isn’t any single product — it’s the story behind all of them. Products grown and made by students on a working campus farm, sold through a student-managed store with over a century of agricultural history. That kind of authenticity is genuinely rare, and it’s something no big-box retailer or Amazon competitor can replicate. Product quality backs that story up — customers consistently highlight the student-made ice cream and estate honey as standout items they tell their friends about. The campus location still brings in a built-in daily audience of students, faculty, and staff, and the Shopify store is already live and ready to be improved rather than rebuilt from scratch.

Current Weaknesses: The biggest issue is awareness. Most students have never been to CPP Farm Store and don’t know it’s open to anyone. Off campus, the problem is even bigger — the store has limited search visibility, an incomplete Google Business Profile, and no presence in retail media channels where potential customers are actively shopping. The online experience doesn’t help either: it’s missing detailed product descriptions, quality photos, and clear pricing. The custom gift box checkout is confusing, there’s no automated order confirmation or pickup update, and there’s basically no post-purchase relationship with customers — no loyalty program, no follow-up email, no way to bring them back.

Market or Environmental Opportunities: Interest in local, sustainable, and mission-driven food has been growing, and farm-to-table branding resonates especially well with the millennial and Gen Z shoppers who make up a big part of the target audience. Seasonal gifting moments like Mother’s Day, graduation, the winter holidays, and campus appreciation events give the store predictable windows of high demand that a focused digital campaign can take full advantage of. Google Shopping and retail media tools are more accessible than ever for small businesses, and the campus itself generates constant event-based foot traffic that, with the right follow-up tools, can be turned into long-term online customers.

Competitive or Operational Challenges: CPP Farm Store competes on two fronts — local farmers’ markets and specialty food retailers with stronger digital presence and easier parking, and national gift retailers like Amazon and Edible Arrangements that capture the gifting budget CPP’s custom boxes should be winning. Operationally, staffing changes with the academic calendar, marketing and tech budgets are limited, and campus infrastructure like parking and wayfinding creates friction that the store can’t fully control.

SWOT Analysis (optional):

Helpful Harmful

4. Ecommerce / Shopify Prototype

4. Ecommerce / Shopify Prototype

Purpose of the Store: The Shopify mini-store extends the CPP Farm Store’s in-person shopping experience online, with a focus on curated gift baskets. The Farm Store’s biggest constraint is that it’s a small, campus-based operation without the staff or budget to run a full online catalog. Rather than trying to replicate the entire in-store inventory, the site centers on a smaller, higher-margin product line that’s easy to browse, customize, and gift. This keeps the build manageable for a small team while still giving the Farm Store a real online presence.

The gift basket focus also solves a specific problem identified in our journey map: customers who want to give the Farm Store as a gift, for a coworker, a professor, a friend off campus, currently have no way to do that without physically visiting the store during limited hours. An online basket store removes that friction while still funneling customers back into the physical location through pickup.

Product Categories Included: Baskets are organized into three groups, each targeting a different customer need identified in our journey analysis:

  • Seasonal baskets, tied to the academic calendar or holidays (finals week, welcome week, harvest season). These create urgency and give the Farm Store a reason to market year-round instead of relying on foot traffic alone.
  • Occasion baskets (thank you, welcome, hostess, get well), aimed at the gifting use case that’s currently unserved.
  • Farm staple bundles, combining produce, honey, jam, and other student-made goods. These lean on the Farm Store’s existing brand strength and give repeat customers a lower-commitment entry point than a themed gift basket.

This structure lets one product line serve three different customer motivations (urgency, gifting, and familiarity) without expanding the catalog into something the team can’t maintain.

Homepage Strategy: A hero banner leads with the current seasonal basket to create urgency and give the homepage a reason to change throughout the year, rather than looking static. Below that, category tiles for the three basket types give both new and returning visitors a fast path to what they’re looking for.

This layout was chosen specifically to shorten the path identified as a pain point in our journey map: customers didn’t have a clear starting point for online discovery. A homepage that immediately branches into “what kind of basket are you here for” solves that in one screen.

Product Page Strategy: Each basket page shows the AI-generated mockup image, a short description, a contents list, and a “customize this basket” option (swap an item, add a note card, choose a size). Personalization is one of the biggest drivers of perceived value in the gift basket category. Without it, the Farm Store’s baskets would look identical to any generic online gift box, which undercuts the “local and personal” positioning that differentiates the Farm Store from competitors like Etsy sellers or national gift basket companies.

Navigation Structure: The top navigation is limited to five items: Shop by Occasion, Shop by Season, Farm Staples, Build Your Own, and About the Farm Store. This was a deliberate constraint. Our journey map showed that first-time visitors were the most likely to bounce if the site felt complicated, so the navigation prioritizes clarity over completeness. “About the Farm Store” is included specifically to link the online store back to the physical location and campus identity, reinforcing that this isn’t a generic ecommerce site.

Customer Experience Improvements: Three features were added directly in response to friction points from the journey map:

  • A local pickup toggle at checkout, since many customers are on or near campus and don’t need shipping
  • A gift note or messaging field, since gifting was identified as the primary unmet need
  • A “text me when ready” notification option, which addresses the uncertainty customers reported about order status when nothing exists online today

How the Prototype Supports Online and Offline Shopping: The site is designed as a discovery and ordering layer that funnels customers into the physical store rather than replacing it. BOPIS (buy online, pick up in store) keeps the in-person visit as part of the experience, which matters for a brand built on community and in-person connection. In-store signage can also point customers to the site for occasions they can’t shop for on the spot, like ordering a basket ahead of time as a gift. This closes the loop between the two channels instead of treating online and in-store as separate businesses, which is the core omnichannel problem this project is meant to solve.

5.7 Recommended Promotion Priority

  1. Raw Honey — promote first; low price point and strongest local story make it the best entry point for a first-time Google Shopping shopper.
  2. Seasonal Produce Box — best suited to retargeting and recurring email/social content tied to what’s currently in season.
  3. Local Gift Basket — reserved for high-intent gifting windows (graduation, finals week, holidays), where a pre-order/pickup model addresses fulfillment constraints.

This staged approach connects directly to the Campaign Plan below — the Gift Basket promotion should peak exactly when the campaign’s seasonal budget (Section 6) is weighted most heavily toward gifting occasions.

6. Campaign Plan

6.1 Business Problem

CPP Farm Store built a custom gift box ordering feature on Shopify, but students, families, and campus organizations largely don’t know it exists. Demand for meaningful, local gifts already exists around graduation, Mother’s Day, and holiday seasons — the Farm Store just isn’t capturing it, so customers default to generic off-campus gift retailers instead.

6.2 Campaign Objective

Drive awareness and online adoption of CPP Farm Store’s custom gift box feature, targeting a 40% increase in gift box orders during the spring peak gifting season (February–May).

Supporting objectives: increase gift-box product page traffic 25% within 60 days of launch, convert at least 3% of that traffic into paying customers, and build repeat purchase behavior for future seasonal occasions.

6.3 Target Audience

Four campus-connected segments, unified by a need for meaningful, convenient local gifts:

  • Local community members — value fresh, locally sourced goods but are often unfamiliar with the store or how to order online.
  • Parents — motivated by milestones like graduation; plan ahead and respond well to email.
  • Alumni — emotionally tied to Cal Poly Pomona; mostly reachable only through digital channels since many live off-campus.
  • Students — the largest and most social-media-engaged segment, but price-sensitive and prone to spontaneous, discount-driven purchases.

6.4 Message Strategy

“Thoughtful Gifts, Grown Here.” The message works across all four segments without needing separate campaigns for each: Thoughtful signals the gift is meaningful rather than generic; Grown Here carries both the literal locally-sourced claim and campus pride. It’s short enough for an Instagram caption, a physical sign, or an email subject line — which matters given how many channels this campaign spans.

6.5 Channel Strategy

Channels were selected to cover the full path from discovery to purchase: Instagram and TikTok (visual awareness, especially for students), Google Search and Google Shopping (capturing existing purchase intent — this is where the product feed in Section 5 becomes active), email (retention and seasonal reminders for parents/alumni), and campus signage, events, and in-store displays (physical touchpoints that funnel back to the same Shopify flow). Retail media (third-party ad networks) was deliberately excluded in favor of concentrating budget on these direct channels.

6.6 Omnichannel Flow

The journey is designed so every stage funnels back to the same Shopify gift-box builder:

Awareness (Instagram/TikTok/signage) → Consideration (Google Search, Shopify landing page, in-store display) → Conversion (Shopify checkout with in-store pickup option) → Retention (email + Google Maps reviews prompting the next seasonal purchase).

This is the same loop the Product Feed (Section 5) is designed to feed into at the Consideration stage, and the same loop the Analytics Dashboard (Section 8) is built to measure end-to-end.

6.7 Budget Recommendation

Total budget: $5,000, weighted toward the highest-intent and highest-reach tactics:

Channel Budget %
Events / Activations $1,500 30%
Email Marketing $1,250 25%
Google Ads $900 18%
In-Store Materials $600 12%
Content Creation $400 8%
Social Media Ads $250 5%
Contingency $100 2%

Events and email receive the largest shares because they reach the campaign’s highest-value segments (families, alumni, campus organizations) directly during peak gifting windows, rather than relying solely on broad-reach paid social.

6.8 KPIs

Success is tracked at each funnel stage — website visits (5,000), product page views (3,000), add-to-cart rate (8%), ecommerce conversion rate (3.0%), repeat purchase rate (20%), and $15,000 in total campaign revenue — with ROAS ($3.50) and CPA ($15 max) as the efficiency guardrails. These same KPIs are what the Analytics Dashboard in Section 8 is built to surface.

6.9 Optimization Plan

Performance is reviewed on a layered cadence: weekly for product-level and traffic anomalies, bi-weekly for creative A/B tests (e.g., “Thoughtful Gifts, Grown Here” vs. seasonal variants) and product-feed accuracy, and monthly for budget reallocation toward whichever channel is outperforming on CPA/ROAS. Spend is also intentionally ramped ahead of Mother’s Day, graduation, and holiday windows and pulled back post-peak, keeping the optimization plan tied to the same seasonal logic driving the budget and product-feed priorities above.

8. Analytics Dashboard

Dashboard Purpose

The CPP Farm Store Omnichannel Performance Dashboard was created to show how the Farm Store could measure the success of its ecommerce and digital marketing efforts. The dashboard brings together customer traffic, product engagement, campaign performance, purchases, revenue, and store pickup interest in one place.

The goal of the dashboard is to help CPP Farm Store move beyond simply measuring website visits and instead understand how customers interact with products and progress toward a purchase.

Because complete live ecommerce data were not available for this project, the dashboard uses simulated data for educational purposes.

Measurement Objectives

The dashboard was designed to help CPP Farm Store:

  • Measure website traffic and customer activity.
  • Track product views and purchase behavior.
  • Compare traffic from different marketing channels.
  • Evaluate campaign performance.
  • Identify products generating customer interest.
  • Measure purchases and revenue.
  • Monitor customer interest in store pickup.
  • Identify opportunities to improve conversion performance.

Data Sources

The current dashboard uses simulated data. In the future, CPP Farm Store could connect the dashboard to real data sources such as:

  • Google Analytics 4 (GA4)
  • Shopify
  • Google Ads
  • Social media platforms
  • Email marketing platforms
  • Point-of-sale or store pickup data

Combining these data sources would allow CPP Farm Store to evaluate both its digital and physical retail activity as part of one omnichannel strategy.

Key Performance Indicators

The dashboard includes several high-level KPIs that summarize customer and ecommerce activity.

  • Total Users: 980
  • Sessions: 1,138
  • Product Views: 796
  • Purchases: 126
  • Revenue: $2,644.00
  • Conversion Rate: 11.07%
  • Average Order Value: $20.98
  • Store Pickup Interest: 65

These KPIs provide a quick overview of how customers move from visiting the website to viewing products and completing purchases.

The conversion rate measures the percentage of sessions that resulted in a purchase, while Average Order Value shows the average revenue generated from each purchase.

Store Pickup Interest is especially important for CPP Farm Store because it helps measure the connection between digital shopping and the physical store.

Dashboard Visualizations

Website Sessions Over Time

The Website Sessions Over Time chart shows changes in website traffic during the selected reporting period. This visualization can help CPP Farm Store identify periods of higher or lower customer activity.

Over time, this information could also be used to determine whether promotions, events, email campaigns, or social media activity resulted in increased website traffic.

Sessions by Marketing Channel

The dashboard compares website sessions generated through several marketing channels, including:

  • Social Media
  • Organic Search
  • Paid Search
  • Email
  • Direct Traffic

This visualization helps CPP Farm Store understand which channels are generating the most website activity and where future marketing resources may be most effective.

Purchases by Campaign

The Purchases by Campaign chart compares the number of purchases generated by different promotional campaigns.

This allows CPP Farm Store to evaluate campaigns based on customer actions rather than only impressions or clicks.

For example, both the Student Appreciation campaign and the Always On campaign generated 28 purchases in the simulated data.

Product Performance

The Product Performance table tracks customer activity for individual products using measures such as:

  • Product Views
  • Add-to-Cart Events
  • Checkout Starts
  • Purchases
  • Revenue

This information can help CPP Farm Store identify products that generate strong customer interest and products that may need improvements to their product pages, pricing, photography, or promotional strategy.

Campaign Performance

The Campaign Performance table compares:

  • Sessions
  • Purchases
  • Revenue
  • Conversion Rate

The simulated results show that the Student Appreciation campaign generated the highest revenue at $800 and achieved a conversion rate of approximately 12.02%.

The Farm Fresh Summer campaign generated the highest number of sessions at 266, but its conversion rate was approximately 9.77%. This demonstrates why traffic alone should not be used to determine campaign success.

CPP Farm Store should evaluate both traffic and conversion performance when deciding where to invest future marketing resources.

Key Insights

Several insights can be identified from the dashboard.

First, the dashboard shows that CPP Farm Store can generate customer traffic from multiple marketing channels. This supports the use of an omnichannel marketing strategy instead of depending on only one source of website traffic.

Second, campaign performance demonstrates that generating more traffic does not always produce the strongest business results. For example, the Farm Fresh Summer campaign generated the most sessions, but other campaigns achieved stronger conversion rates.

Third, product-level data can help CPP Farm Store identify where customers move from viewing a product to adding it to their cart, beginning checkout, and completing a purchase. Products with strong views but weaker purchase activity may require changes to their product descriptions, photography, pricing, or calls to action.

Finally, the Store Pickup Interest metric demonstrates an important opportunity to connect ecommerce with the physical CPP Farm Store. Customers could discover products online and then visit the Farm Store to complete or collect their purchase.

Recommended Actions

Based on the dashboard, CPP Farm Store should consider the following actions:

  1. Monitor both traffic and conversions.
    Marketing channels and campaigns should not be evaluated only by the number of sessions they generate. Purchases, revenue, and conversion rate should also be considered.

  2. Invest in high-performing campaigns.
    Campaigns that consistently generate strong conversion rates and revenue should receive additional attention and marketing resources.

  3. Improve product pages when necessary.
    Products that receive strong views but fewer purchases should be reviewed for improvements to photography, product descriptions, pricing information, and calls to action.

  4. Promote store pickup.
    CPP Farm Store should clearly communicate store pickup options throughout the ecommerce experience to strengthen the connection between online shopping and the physical store.

  5. Use the dashboard for ongoing optimization.
    Management should regularly review dashboard performance and adjust marketing campaigns, product promotions, and ecommerce strategies based on customer behavior.

Data Limitations

The main limitation of the dashboard is that it uses simulated data rather than complete live CPP Farm Store ecommerce data.

For this reason, the dashboard should be viewed as a recommended measurement framework rather than a representation of actual CPP Farm Store financial performance.

Additional limitations include:

  • No long-term historical ecommerce data.
  • Limited marketing attribution across multiple channels.
  • Store Pickup Interest does not necessarily represent completed pickups.
  • Simulated revenue does not represent actual Farm Store revenue.
  • Customer behavior may change when a fully operational ecommerce store is launched.

Once CPP Farm Store has access to live ecommerce data, the simulated information could be replaced with data from GA4, Shopify, advertising platforms, and store operations.

10. Conclusion

10. Conclusion

CPP Farm Store has an opportunity to strengthen its omnichannel retail strategy by creating a more connected experience between its physical location and digital channels.

Throughout this project, the team developed recommendations across the customer journey, Shopify ecommerce prototype, product feed, campaign strategy, creative assets, and analytics dashboard. These components work together to create a more complete customer experience rather than operating as separate marketing activities.

The Shopify prototype provides customers with a way to discover CPP Farm Store products online, while an optimized product feed can improve product visibility across digital platforms. Campaigns and creative content can increase awareness and encourage customers to interact with the Farm Store beyond a single physical visit.

The analytics dashboard supports these recommendations by giving CPP Farm Store a framework for measuring customer behavior and marketing performance. Metrics such as sessions, product views, purchases, revenue, conversion rate, Average Order Value, campaign performance, and store pickup interest can help management determine what is working and where improvements are needed.

One of the strongest opportunities is the connection between ecommerce and store pickup. Allowing customers to discover products online while encouraging pickup at the physical Farm Store combines the convenience of digital shopping with the unique in-person experience of CPP Farm Store.

Overall, the proposed omnichannel strategy provides CPP Farm Store with a practical foundation for future ecommerce growth. By gradually implementing these recommendations, connecting real customer data, and continuously measuring performance, CPP Farm Store can improve customer convenience, increase product visibility, strengthen marketing effectiveness, and create a more connected retail experience for the Cal Poly Pomona community and surrounding customers.
